**FINDINGS**

1. **Section F (Round 3).** Section F treats the assistant's answer to "which vendors it did not include and why" as vetting the set. That confuses an explanation with a record. The answer is a retrospective account written by the same tool that built the set, after the set exists. Nothing requires the full candidate list to be recorded before screening. Nothing requires each cut to be recorded as it is made, with its criterion and ground, or the not-offered answer to be labelled as proposed and verified by the Human before it enters the record. As drafted, the field of vendors is defined and certified by the producing tool.

2. **Test 3, waiver authority (Round 4).** Test 3 gives its source as law and assigns its waiver to the Owner "if the timeline requires." A constraint sourced in law is not the Owner's to waive. The waiver condition is also a hedge, since no test defines when the timeline "requires" it. The Owner is also the Reviewer, so one person can waive the only constraint, under a condition they judge, and then confirm the result.

3. **Test 3, self-certification (Round 1).** Test 3 states that signatures "satisfy the eIDAS requirements for advanced electronic signatures" but requires no evidence. Compare test 4, which requires vendor documentation, and test 2, which requires a linked source. As written, the assistant can satisfy test 3 by asserting it. Whether a product meets the advanced signature requirements is also a legal assessment, not an observation. The draft does not say who is competent to confirm it.

4. **Round 2, salience.** Every test describes something the finished recommendation will visibly contain: a list, a residency statement, a compliance claim, an integration citation, a named runner-up. Test 4's provenance clause comes closest to targeting an assumption, but it still specifies a visible citation rather than a failure mode. This definition was written by salience.

5. **Gap, hostile reviewer (Round 2).** The draft's intended reader is procurement. No test covers what procurement, security or legal would likely check first. That includes security certifications or attestations, a data processing agreement, contract term and termination rights, and liability terms. Which of these procurement actually requires is Unknown, but no test reaches any of them.

6. **Gap, migration and exit (Round 2, Round 5).** The stated stakes are "a year of contract and a migration." No test reaches migration effort or exit: export of signed documents and audit trails, format portability, or the cost of leaving at renewal. This is the least discussed element with the largest consequence if wrong. It is also a non-recoverable error class: loss of a signed document's audit trail on exit is not prohibited anywhere.

7. **Test 5, elimination record (Round 3).** Test 5 asks for the runner-up and "why it lost." The runner-up is not necessarily the strongest discard. The discards made while cutting the market down to three are never named. No test requires an elimination order, grounds, or the information state at each cut. No test distinguishes infeasibility (failing test 3) or dominance from preference. No test requires the strongest discard to be resurrected at confirmation. "Why it lost" can be written after the fact by the tool.

8. **Gap, basis of the recommendation (Round 4).** No test states what the recommendation turns on. Pricing, residency and integration are classified as preferences, but none has a threshold, weight or ordering. The implicit settlement readable in the tests privileges documentary evidence and the one legal constraint. It subordinates cost, migration, user experience and contract terms. No subordinated criterion has a floor: no budget ceiling, no minimum integration scope, no acceptable residency.

9. **Test 2, pass regardless of answer (Round 1).** Test 2 passes once residency is stated and linked, whatever the residency is. It is observable but not a test of consequence. Whether residency is a preference or a constraint under internal policy or data protection obligations is Unknown. If it is a constraint, Section B misclassifies it, and its source and waiver authority are missing.

10. **Test 1, pricing as a point estimate (Round 1, Round 5).** Test 1 requires "annual pricing for twenty users" with no source, unlike test 2, so the assistant can self-report it. A single annual figure carries no uncertainty band and no basis. The draft does not distinguish list price from quoted price, or account for overage or volume limits, add-ons required for integration, or user growth over the contract year.

11. **Test 1, count anchor (Round 1).** "Three vendors" is a count chosen without a stated consequence. Nothing ties the size of the shortlist to the size of the feasible field. If fewer than three vendors pass test 3, test 1 pushes toward including an infeasible one. If more pass, the cut to three is the unrecorded cut in finding 7.

12. **Test 4, undefined terms (Round 1).** Test 4 does not name "our document system" and does not define what "integration" must do. Vendor documentation of an integration confirms a claim, not a working fit with the team's actual workflow. The test passes on a documented connector that may not cover the team's use.

13. **Gap, amendment path (Round 1).** No recorded path exists for amending this definition mid-task. One example is when the market scan shows that no vendor meets test 3 at the expected price.

14. **Roles (Round 1).** The Owner and Reviewer are the same person. That person is distinct from the Builder (the assistant). Whether Tier 2 requires a further separation is Unknown. Procurement, which receives the purchase request, has no confirming role in the definition.

15. **Gap, affected parties (Round 2).** The definition does not mention external signers and counterparties, whose documents depend on signature validity. It also does not mention legal or compliance, IT or security, or the twenty end users.

16. **Gap, availability and vendor tail (Round 5).** No test bounds worst-case availability: service level, outage history, or vendor continuity over the contract year. Every quality dimension the draft touches is a single stated attribute, with no worst-case bound beside it.
