### Turn 1, assistant (stop_reason end_turn)

**INTERROGATION OF: Recommend an e-signature vendor from an assistant-compiled shortlist, as the basis for a procurement purchase request | Tier: 2 | Date: not supplied**

**FINDINGS**

1. **Section F (option set vetting).** The definition treats the assistant's answer about which vendors it did not include as the thing that makes the set "vetted." This confuses an explanation with a record. The answer is a retrospective account produced by the same tool that built the set, so it can describe the gaps only as the tool now chooses to describe them. The draft does not require:
   - the full candidate list to be recorded before any screening,
   - each cut to be recorded as it is made, with its criterion and ground, or
   - the retrospective account to be labelled as proposed and verified by a Human before it enters the record.

   As written, the tool compiles the field, screens it and certifies its own screening. Who outside the assistant sees the field before it narrows to three?

2. **Test 3 (eIDAS constraint), waiver authority.** The source is recorded as law, but the waiver is assigned to the Owner. An Owner cannot waive a legal requirement, and timeline pressure is not a ground for doing so. What happens if the only vendor that fits the timeline issues signatures that are not advanced electronic signatures?

3. **Test 3, who checks.** The test does not say who confirms satisfaction, or against what evidence. As drafted, the assistant could satisfy it by asserting that each vendor meets the requirements. Is a vendor's own compliance claim enough, or does the test require something the Human can check independently?

4. **Test 3, applicability and level.** The test assumes eIDAS is the governing regime and that "advanced" is the right level. Neither is established in the task description. What if some signature types or counterparties need qualified signatures, or the team signs under a jurisdiction where eIDAS does not apply? See UNKNOWNS.

5. **Roles (Round 1).**
   - The team lead is both Owner and Reviewer. The draft does not say whether Tier 2 permits this.
   - No Approver is named, although procurement receives the output.
   - No amendment path is recorded. If a criterion turns out to be wrong mid-task, for example residency proves to be a constraint, who may change the definition, and where is the change recorded?

6. **Test 5 (runner-up), and Round 3.** Naming the runner-up and why it lost is a partial elimination record for one option only. It does not require:
   - the order of cuts,
   - the grounds for each cut, distinguishing infeasibility, dominance and preference, or
   - the information state at each cut.

   The runner-up is also not necessarily the strongest discard. The draft has no requirement to name the strongest discard and resurrect it at confirmation. A vendor cut early on a weak ground would never surface.

7. **Test 1 (pricing): self-reportable.** Test 2 requires a linked source, but Test 1 does not. The assistant could state pricing figures that no one can trace.

8. **Test 1 (pricing): what the figure means.** The test fixes "annual pricing for twenty users" without saying whether that means list price, a quote, or a price including signature-volume limits, overages or tier thresholds.

9. **Test 1 (pricing): a count, not a consequence.** "Three vendors" is anchored to a count. Why three, and what if the field contains only two feasible vendors, or seven?

10. **Migration and exit (Round 2, least discussed element).** The stated stakes are a year of contract and a migration. No test reaches:
    - exit cost,
    - export of signed documents and audit trails,
    - long-term validation of signatures after the contract ends, or
    - contract term and renewal conditions.

    This is the least discussed element with the largest consequence if wrong, and no test touches it.

11. **Hostile reviewer coverage (Round 2).** No test covers what procurement, legal or security would check first: security certifications, the data processing agreement, liability terms, and vendor viability. If none of these matter here, the draft does not say so.

12. **Absent affected parties (Round 2).** The following appear nowhere in the definition:
    - external signers (customers and counterparties), who absorb any usability or validity failure,
    - the twenty end users,
    - legal or compliance,
    - data protection, and
    - IT, who own the document system integration.

13. **Salience (Round 2).**
    - Tests 1, 2, 3 and 5 each describe something the finished recommendation will visibly contain.
    - Test 4 is the only test that targets an assumption, namely that the assistant's summary is accurate. Even Test 4 is satisfied by a visible citation unless someone checks it.
    - No test targets an absence or a tail.

    The definition is close to having been written by salience.

14. **Test 2 (data residency).** "Stated" tests presence, not adequacy. Three issues follow:
    - No test says what residency is acceptable, so a vendor storing data anywhere passes.
    - Residency is classified as a preference in Section B. If the team's data is subject to transfer restrictions, it may be a constraint. See UNKNOWNS.
    - A linked source page can change. No retrieval date is required, and no one is named to confirm that the page says what the recommendation claims.

15. **Test 4 (integration).**
    - "Confirmed by the vendor's documentation" does not say who performs the confirmation. If the assistant reads the documentation and reports that it confirms integration, that is the assistant's summary again.
    - Documentation describes claimed capability, not a working integration with this team's configuration.
    - "Our document system" is not named in the task description.

16. **Section B (classification).** Section B classifies Tests 1, 2, 4 and 5 as preferences. These are output requirements rather than criteria, so the vendor selection criteria themselves are never stated or classified. What is the recommendation optimising? None of the preferences has a floor: no price ceiling, no minimum acceptable residency, no minimum integration depth. Without floors, a vendor can be subordinated to zero on any of them.

17. **Implicit settlement (Round 4).** The tests privilege three things:
    - price, the only quantified criterion,
    - legal form of the signature, and
    - documentary provenance.

    They subordinate usability, signer experience, support, security, reliability and exit cost by omission. The settlement favours the team lead's timeline and procurement's cost comparison. If the subordinated criteria turn out to matter, the cost falls on signers, end users and whoever runs the migration next year.

18. **Tail tests (Round 5).** Availability and support appear nowhere, so no worst-case bound exists for outages during signing.

19. **Non-recoverable errors (Round 5).** Two error classes have non-recoverable consequences:
    - a signature later held invalid, and
    - signed documents or audit trails that cannot be exported at exit.

    Neither is named or prohibited anywhere in the work. Test 3 covers only the first, and only at selection.

20. **Uncertainty bands (Round 5).** The pricing figures in Test 1 will need an uncertainty band and a basis: renewal increases, user growth and volume overages. No test requires them. The task is not stated to recur, so no finding on sample size or run-to-run variance.

**UNKNOWNS**

- **Date.** Not supplied. Closes with the interrogation date.
- **Jurisdiction and signature level.** Whether eIDAS governs the team's signing, and whether any document class requires qualified rather than advanced signatures. Closes with the jurisdictions of the team and its counterparties, and legal's view of the signature level required.
- **Data residency.** Whether residency is legally constrained for this team's data. Closes with the data classification and any applicable transfer rules.
- **Role separation.** Whether Tier 2 permits the Owner and Reviewer to be the same person, and who the Approver is. Closes with the tier's role rules and the named Approver, if any.
- **Document system.** Which document system the integration must work with. Closes with its name and version.
- **Procurement requirements.** Whether procurement has mandatory requirements of its own, such as security questionnaires, contract terms or a minimum number of bids. Closes with procurement's intake criteria.

**THE ONE QUESTION**

Before the assistant compiles the field, who other than the assistant will record and check the full candidate list and the ground for every cut, so that the shortlist rests on a record rather than on the tool's own account of what it left out?

**CLOSE**

The findings are questions, not fixes. The pen returns to the Human.
