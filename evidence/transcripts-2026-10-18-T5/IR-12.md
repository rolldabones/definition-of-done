**FINDINGS**

1. **Section F (option set vetting).** The definition treats the assistant's answer to "which vendors did you not include and why" as what makes the set vetted. That answer is a retrospective explanation, not a record. Nothing requires the full candidate list to be recorded before screening, or each cut to be recorded as it is made with its criterion and ground. Nothing labels the not-included answer as proposed or requires verification before it enters the record. As written, the tool that built the set also certifies its completeness. Who checks the not-included answer against anything other than itself?

2. **Test 3 (waiver authority).** The constraint's source is law, yet the draft assigns its waiver to the Owner. A team lead cannot waive a legal requirement. "If the timeline requires" also subordinates a legal constraint to schedule pressure. Who actually holds waiver authority for this requirement, and should a schedule be a ground for waiving it at all?

3. **Test 3 (self-certification and composite).** "Satisfy the eIDAS requirements for advanced electronic signatures" bundles several distinct requirements into one pass or fail. No test names the evidence, its source, or who checks it. Unlike tests 2 and 4, test 3 has no provenance requirement, so the assistant could pass it by assertion. Is the Human confirming each requirement against a primary source, or accepting a summary?

4. **Test 3 (premise).** The draft assumes advanced electronic signatures are the governing legal threshold. Whether this team's documents need simple, advanced or qualified signatures, and in which jurisdictions, is not stated. If the threshold is wrong in either direction, test 3 either excludes viable vendors or passes inadequate ones. (See Unknowns.)

5. **Round 3 gap (elimination record).** Test 5 records why one option lost. Nothing records the order of cuts, their grounds, or the information state at each cut for the other candidates. Nothing distinguishes cuts made for infeasibility or dominance from cuts made for preference. Nothing requires the strongest discard to be named and resurrected at confirmation. The runner-up is the second-best survivor, not necessarily the strongest discard.

6. **Roles (Round 1).** The team lead is both Owner and Reviewer, and no Approver is named. Is a separate confirmer required at Tier 2? Procurement is named as an audience but holds no role. Who confirms each test, and who signs the definition as met?

7. **Amendment path (Round 1).** No recorded path exists for changing this definition mid-task. This matters most for test 3, where the draft already anticipates timeline pressure. Without a path, the change would happen informally.

8. **Test 1 (anchored to form, self-reportable).** "Three vendors" is a count, not a consequence. Why three, and what happens if only two pass test 3? "Annual pricing for twenty users" has no stated source, so list price, quoted price and assistant estimate would all pass. Nothing covers renewal escalation, minimum terms or seat-band thresholds near twenty.

9. **Salience (Round 2).** Every Section A test describes something the finished recommendation will visibly contain: a list, prices, residency statements, a compliance claim, integration confirmation, a runner-up. The one element aimed at an absence (Section F) is a self-report. This definition was written by salience.

10. **Hostile reviewer and largest-consequence element (Round 2).** The stakes name a year of contract and a migration. Yet no test reaches:
    - exit terms,
    - export of signed documents and audit trails,
    - termination or notice periods,
    - retention of evidentiary records after the contract ends.

    These are what procurement or legal would check first. Signed-document portability is the least discussed element with the largest consequence if wrong, and nothing in the definition reaches it.

11. **Absent affected parties (Round 2).** These parties appear nowhere in the definition:
    - external signers, whose experience and legal position depend on the vendor,
    - IT or security,
    - legal or compliance,
    - data subjects whose personal data passes through the vendor.

12. **Test 2 (stated, not judged).** The test requires residency to be stated and sourced, but not that it meets any requirement. A vendor with unacceptable residency passes. Is the source a marketing page or a contractual commitment such as a data processing agreement? These can differ.

13. **Test 4 (undefined "integration" and stale evidence).** "Integration" is not defined: native connector, API, or manual export? The test does not require the documentation to be current or to apply to the team's version of the document system. Who checks that the documentation says what the recommendation claims?

14. **Section B (preferences without weight or floor, Round 4).** Tests 1, 2, 4 and 5 are classified as preferences with no ranking, weighting or floor. The implicit settlement privileges legal compliance, then price. It subordinates residency, integration, exit cost, support and signer experience. If the cheapest compliant vendor has poor integration, nothing prevents its selection. The team lead benefits from a simple decision. The organisation and its signers absorb the cost if a subordinated criterion turns out to matter.

15. **Non-recoverable error classes (Round 5).** Two errors are not prohibited anywhere in the work: signatures that fail legal validity on executed agreements, and personal data held outside permitted jurisdictions. Test 3 bounds the vendor at selection, but nothing addresses evidentiary failure or residency breach in use.

16. **Uncertainty (Round 5).** The work will contain prices, possibly migration effort, and residency and compliance claims. No test requires an uncertainty band or basis for any number, or a date for any vendor claim. Price is presented as a point figure. Round 5 run-to-run variance: no finding, as the task does not appear to recur.

**UNKNOWNS**

- **Legal threshold:** which signature level the team's documents legally require, and in which jurisdictions. Input: a statement from legal or compliance of the required level and governing law.
- **Waiver authority for test 3:** who in the organisation can approve departure from a legal requirement. Input: the name or role holding that authority.
- **Tier 2 role requirements:** whether Tier 2 requires the Reviewer or Approver to be distinct from the Owner. Input: the applicable tier rules.
- **Procurement's own requirements:** whether procurement has mandatory criteria (security questionnaires, contract terms, vendor onboarding rules) that should sit in the definition. Input: procurement's requirements list.
- **Document system:** which system and version test 4 refers to. Input: its identity and version.
- **Residency requirement:** whether any residency constraint exists that test 2 should be judged against. Input: the data protection position for the documents to be signed.
- **Date:** not supplied.
