**FINDINGS**

1. **Section F (Round 3).** The definition treats the assistant's account of which vendors it did not include as the vetting of the set. This confuses an explanation with a record. The tool that built the set is the same tool explaining its omissions, so the explanation cannot validate the set. The draft also has three gaps:
   - Nothing requires the full candidate list to be recorded before any screening.
   - Nothing requires each cut to be recorded as it is made, with its criterion and ground.
   - Nothing labels the not-offered answer as proposed, or requires a Human to verify it before it enters the record.

   What would let the team lead or procurement detect a credible vendor that never appeared at all?

2. **Test 3, waiver authority (Round 4).** The draft names law as the source of this constraint but assigns the waiver to the Owner. The Owner cannot waive a legal requirement. Who actually holds that authority, and is that person named anywhere?

3. **Test 3, waiver trigger (Round 1).** "If the timeline requires" is an undefined condition, not a pass or fail threshold. Who decides the timeline requires it, against what date, and is that decision recorded?

4. **Test 3, self-certification (Round 1).** Test 2 requires a linked source and Test 4 rejects the assistant's summary. Test 3 requires neither. As written, the assistant can satisfy it by stating that each vendor meets the advanced electronic signature requirements. What evidence must be produced, and who other than the assistant checks it?

5. **Migration and exit gap (Round 2).** The task description names a migration as part of the cost of a wrong choice. No test reaches any of the following:
   - migration cost or effort;
   - data and audit-trail export on exit;
   - contract term and termination conditions;
   - the fate of already-signed documents if the vendor is replaced.

   This is the least discussed element with the largest stated consequence. Which test reaches it?

6. **Roles (Round 1).** The team lead is both Owner and Reviewer, and no confirmer is named for the finished work. Does Tier 2 require the person confirming the definition and the work to be distinct from the person who set the definition? No amendment path exists if a test proves wrong mid-task, for example if no vendor passes Test 3. Who may amend, and where is the amendment recorded?

7. **Test 5, elimination record (Round 3).** The test requires the runner-up and why it lost. It does not require:
   - the order in which vendors were eliminated;
   - the grounds and the information state at each cut;
   - any distinction between infeasibility (failing Test 3), dominance, and preference.

8. **Test 5, strongest discard (Round 3).** The runner-up is a finalist, not necessarily the strongest discard. Is the strongest discarded candidate required to be named and resurrected at confirmation? A loss explanation written after the choice is also a retrospective account. Is it labeled and verified?

9. **Test 1 (Rounds 1 and 5).** "Three vendors" is anchored to a count rather than to consequence. Why three, and what happens if fewer than three pass Test 3? "Annual pricing for twenty users" does not state its basis:
   - list or quoted;
   - which tier or plan;
   - whether add-ons, overage, or envelope limits are included;
   - whether a price is required for the full contract term or only for year one.

   No uncertainty band or source is required for the figure, although Test 2 requires a source for residency.

10. **Salience (Round 2).** Each test is satisfied by content the finished document will visibly contain: a list, a linked statement, a compliance assertion, a documentation reference, a named runner-up. Test 4 guards an assumption in spirit, but it still passes on visible content. This definition was written by salience.

11. **Absent parties (Round 2).** The definition does not mention:
    - the external counterparties who will sign through the chosen tool;
    - legal or compliance;
    - data protection;
    - IT or security, who will own the integration;
    - procurement's own mandatory criteria, although procurement is a named audience.

    Who among these absorbs the consequence if the choice is wrong?

12. **Hostile reviewer (Round 2).** Procurement, as a competent hostile reviewer, would plausibly check first:
    - contract terms and auto-renewal;
    - security certifications;
    - vendor viability;
    - whether the quotes are comparable.

    No test covers any of these.

13. **Preferences without floors (Round 4).** Section B classifies everything other than Test 3 as a preference, which raises three problems:
    - Data residency (Test 2) and integration (Test 4) are recorded but have no floor. A vendor with unacceptable residency or no working integration could still be recommended.
    - No weighting tells the reader how price, residency, and integration trade against each other.
    - Tests 1 and 5 are process requirements, not selection criteria, so classifying them as preferences blurs what is being traded.

14. **Implicit settlement (Round 4).** The tests privilege documentary verifiability and legal form. They subordinate migration cost, exit, usability for signatories, and total cost of ownership. The waiver clause in Test 3 privileges the timeline over the legal requirement. The team lead benefits from that waiver. The organization and its counterparties absorb the risk if signatures are later challenged.

15. **Test 4 (Round 1).** "Confirmed by the vendor's documentation" does not say who performs the confirmation. Documentation describing an integration is also not evidence that the integration works with this team's configuration. The document system is not named.

16. **Non-recoverable errors and tails (Round 5).** These error classes are not prohibited outright:
    - a signature later found legally insufficient;
    - signed records stored outside a permitted jurisdiction;
    - loss of audit trails on migration.

    The only relevant test (Test 3) is waivable. No test addresses worst-case pricing such as renewal uplift or overage, only a single annual figure.
